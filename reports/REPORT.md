# Demand Forecasting & Inventory Backtest

_Generated by `make backtest`._ 200 SKU-warehouse series,
weekly data from Jan 2012 to Dec 2016.
Forecasts are made 13 weeks ahead from 26 Sep 2016 using only earlier data.

## Accuracy

| model                    | WAPE   | Bias   |   MASE | fill_rate   |   avg_inventory_units |
|:-------------------------|:-------|:-------|-------:|:------------|----------------------:|
| Moving average (8w)      | 48.7%  | -20.3% |   0.76 | 98.0%       |            34,715,878 |
| Global gradient boosting | 51.1%  | -3.4%  |   0.75 | 98.8%       |            42,391,749 |
| Holt-Winters             | 54.2%  | 1.4%   |   0.84 | 99.3%       |            48,950,988 |
| Seasonal naive           | 67.0%  | -4.6%  |   1.01 | 99.0%       |            58,149,028 |

- **WAPE**: total absolute error as a share of total demand (lower is better)
- **Bias**: positive means over-forecasting
- **MASE**: error relative to a same-week-last-year forecast, median across series (below 1 beats it)

## From accuracy to inventory

Each model drives a weekly order-up-to policy with a 2-week lead time and safety stock sized
for a 95% service target from that model's own historical error.

![tradeoff](tradeoff.png)

- **Most accurate:** Moving average (8w) (WAPE 48.7%).
- **Recommended for planning:** **Moving average (8w)**, which meets the 95% service target
  (98.0% fill rate) with the least inventory, **40% less** than the most
  stock-heavy model.
- A simple baseline wins here: with lumpy, intermittent demand, extra model complexity mostly adds variance. Worth knowing before investing in ML forecasting.
